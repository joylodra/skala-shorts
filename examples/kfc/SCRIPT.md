# "The Chicken Shop With No Chicken" — Skala short #1 (draft v1)

Audience: brand owners, brand managers, social media managers, creators.
Length: ~217 words ≈ 59 s at 3.7 words/s. Vertical 1080x1920, cartoon-3d-short style, office setting, Skala as the brand manager.

## Facts (with sources)
- 14 Feb 2018: KFC UK switched its distribution from Bidvest Logistics to DHL (with QSL), run from a single depot in Rugby. — Fortune, Marketing Week, The Drum
- By Mon 19 Feb, ~562–575 of ~900 UK restaurants were closed (closures began over the weekend of 17–18 Feb). — Fortune (citing BBC)
- 20 Feb 2018: Tower Hamlets police (London) tweeted asking people to stop contacting them about the #KFCCrisis; it is not a police matter. — The Week, Yahoo UK
- 23 Feb 2018: full-page ad in The Sun and Metro by agency Mother London: an empty bucket with the letters rearranged to "FCK", copy opening "We're sorry." KFC did not blame DHL publicly. — HuffPost UK, LBB, Marketing Week
- The ad won three gold Lions and one silver at Cannes Lions 2018. — WARC, The Drum
- KFC later returned deliveries for ~350 outlets (northern England, Scotland) to Bidvest. — Marketing Week / aboutresilience

## Script
| # | Beat | Voice-over | Picture (draft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | Britain's biggest chicken chain ran out of chicken. | Skala at a desk, opens an empty chicken bucket. Flash. |
| 2 | Setting | On Valentine's Day 2018, KFC handed every delivery to a brand-new partner. One single warehouse would now feed nine hundred restaurants. | Wide office "war room": big wall map, 900 little lights on. One warehouse icon, trucks fanning out. |
| 3 | Action | Within days, the trucks were late. On day three, restaurants started closing. By day five, more than half of them were shut. | Trucks stuck in a jam on the map; lights on the map blink out in waves. Skala's coffee goes cold. |
| 4 | Action+ | Fans were so upset, some of them called the police. A London police team had to tweet that a closed KFC is not a police matter. | Phones ringing all over the office; a big phone-screen card with a police badge and a tweet bubble. |
| 5 | Snap | A chicken shop. With no chicken. The whole country was laughing. | The last light on the map goes out. Office lights flicker. Skala's shocked face. |
| 6 | Deadpan | Luckily, nobody got hurt. Unluckily, everybody got hungry. | Cut to a sad queue at a closed shop door; a stomach growl SFX. |
| 7 | Turn | Most brands would hide behind a careful corporate statement. KFC bought a full-page newspaper ad instead: an empty bucket, with its own three letters shuffled to spell F-C-K. Underneath: We're sorry. | Skala crumples a long "corporate statement" draft. Newspaper spins in; letters on the bucket hop and swap places. |
| 8 | Explanation | Why did it work? Think of spilling coffee on your shirt before a big meeting. Hide it, and everyone stares. Point at it and laugh first, and everyone laughs with you. | Skala spills coffee in a meeting room. Version A: hides it, coworkers stare. Version B: points and laughs, the room laughs. |
| 9 | Consequence | The apology was shared all over the world, and won three gold Lions at Cannes, the biggest awards in advertising. In a crisis, don't hide. Own it, in your own voice. | Three gold trophies drop onto Skala's desk; Skala celebrates. |
| 10 | Callback | And that brand-new delivery partner? For hundreds of restaurants, KFC brought the old one back. | Map: the old trucks roll back in. Skala gives a deadpan look to camera. |

## Notes
- No real logos: a generic red-and-white bucket and newspaper. The letters K-F-C appear only for the joke.
- No real people depicted; Skala and generic coworkers act it out.
